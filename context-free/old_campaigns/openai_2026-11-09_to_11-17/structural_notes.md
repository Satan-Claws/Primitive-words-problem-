# Structural route: proved lemmas and limits

These results do **not** settle whether the primitive language is context-free.
They apply without any ambiguity assumption.

## 1. Polynomial rectangle covers across a balanced interval

Fix an alphabet of size s >= 2, a length n >= 1, and an interval I of m
positions with m <= 2n/3. Represent a word by its outside context x in
Sigma^(n-m) and its interval filling y in Sigma^m, preserving position order.
A rectangle means an arbitrary Cartesian product A x B in this partition.
Neither A nor B is required to be generated by a small grammar.

**Lemma.** Q intersect Sigma^n is a union of at most

    [s(s-1)n]^4 (sm+1) <= [s(s-1)]^4 (s+1)n^5

primitive-only rectangles in this partition. For binary words the last
bound is 48n^5.

**Proof.** Every nonprimitive word is a p-th power for some prime p dividing
n. First consider prime exponents p >= 11. For a fixed outside context x,
at most one interval filling produces such a power.

Indeed, one of the two contiguous pieces outside I has length
ell >= (n-m)/2 >= n/6. A p-th power has period d=n/p and d <= n/11 < ell,
so its letters in that piece determine the whole n-letter word uniquely.
For two different primes p,q >= 11, suppose two such powers agree on the
outside context. Their shared contiguous piece has periods d=n/p and
e=n/q. Since p and q are distinct primes dividing n,

    gcd(d,e) = n/(pq),
    d+e-gcd(d,e) = n(1/p+1/q-1/(pq)) <= 23n/143 < n/6 <= ell.

The maximum in the inequality is attained at p=11,q=13. Fine-Wilf gives
period g=gcd(d,e) on the shared piece. As its length is at least d and e,
it contains a full period block of each power, with each such block
g-periodic. Because g divides both d and e, both full words are the same
g-periodic extension of the shared piece. This proves uniqueness.

Let f(x) be this unique filling when it exists. The complement of the graph
of this partial function is covered by the following sm+1 rectangles:

* {x: f(x) is undefined} x Sigma^m;
* for j=1,...,m and a in Sigma,
  {x: f(x) is defined and f(x)_j=a} x {y: y_j != a}.

This complement is exactly the words which are not p-th powers for any
prime p >= 11.

For each of the four remaining primes p in {2,3,5,7}, if p does not divide
n the non-p-th-powers form the universal rectangle. Otherwise set d=n/p.
A word fails to be a p-th power iff w_i != w_(i+d) for some i <= n-d.
Each particular mismatch is covered by one rectangle if both positions
lie on the same side of the partition, or by s(s-1) rectangles if they
lie on opposite sides (specify the unequal letters). Thus non-p-th-powers
have a cover of size at most s(s-1)n. Intersect these four covers and the
partial-function-complement cover. Intersections of rectangles are
rectangles, and the resulting intersection is exactly Q at length n.
This establishes the displayed bound. The argument includes absent prime
divisors and m=0; the latter case only needs the first rectangle. Q here
is restricted to n>=1, so epsilon is not admitted. QED.

**Consequence and limitation.** A superpolynomial lower bound for the
number of unrestricted rectangles across these interval/context partitions
is impossible. This rules out one naive grammar-size lower-bound route.
It does not give a polynomial-size CFG. The predicates defining A and B
above may themselves be complicated, and the covers have no demonstrated
compatibility across lengths or recursive spans. A CFG lower bound would
have to exploit those additional requirements.

## 2. Sharper midpoint statement

Let n>=2, m=floor(n/2), and split into prefix x of length m and suffix y
of length n-m. For a fixed x there is at most one completion which is a
p-th power for an odd prime p.

For a fixed odd p the period n/p is <= m and hence determines the
completion. For two distinct odd p,q, their Fine-Wilf threshold is at most
7n/15 (the extremal pair is 3,5). Such a pair requires n>=15, and then
floor(n/2)>=7n/15. The preceding proof therefore applies.

If n is odd, every nonprimitive completion comes from an odd prime; hence
there are at most s ceil(n/2)+1 primitive-only rectangles. If n is even,
there is one square completion y=x and at most one odd-prime completion.
For rows having the latter completion, certify inequality with each of
the two forbidden suffixes by specifying two mismatch positions and their
letters. For rows without it, certify inequality only with x. This gives
at most s^2 m^2 + sm rectangles. The two forbidden completions can really
be distinct: prefix aba at n=6 has completions abaaba and ababab.

## 3. Puncturing lemma

**Lemma.** Two distinct nonprimitive words of the same positive length
cannot differ in exactly one position. The alphabet can be arbitrary.

**Proof.** Suppose w and v have proper divisor periods d,e of n and agree
outside position i. Let l=lcm(d,e). If l<n, position i+l modulo n differs
from i. Periodicity and equality at that position force w_i=v_i.

If l=n, let g=gcd(d,e). Construct a bipartite graph with vertices for
residue classes modulo d on the left and modulo e on the right. Each
position j contributes an edge between its two residue classes. By the
Chinese remainder theorem, each component is the complete bipartite graph
K_(d/g,e/g), with one edge per compatible residue pair because l=n.
Both side sizes are at least 2: if one were 1, one period would divide the
other, contradicting l=n with both periods proper. Label the left vertices
by the corresponding letters of w, and the right vertices by those of v.
Every edge except possibly i joins equal labels. Deleting one edge from a
complete bipartite graph with both sides of size >=2 leaves its endpoints
connected by a path of length three. Hence those endpoints have equal
labels too, giving w_i=v_i, a contradiction. QED.

**Run-length consequence.** Fix a positive cyclic binary run-length vector
and vary just one coordinate over positive integers, keeping all other
coordinates fixed. At most one resulting binary word is nonprimitive.
Choose a run boundary as origin. A nonprimitive binary word containing
both letters has a root boundary at a run boundary, so its entire cyclic
run-length vector is a proper power, with an even number of runs per root.
Two bad choices would give two imprimitive vectors of equal vector length
differing in one coordinate, contrary to the lemma (use positive integers
as the symbols). The reverse implication between vector imprimitivity and
word imprimitivity is not asserted: an odd run-vector period can interchange
the two letters, as the vector (1,1) for the primitive word ab illustrates.

## 4. Audit of finite_checks.py

The `minimum_cnf` encoding correctly minimizes the **number of
nonterminals**, not the number of productions or total symbol size, among
strict epsilon-free CNF grammars generating exactly Q intersect
{a,b}^{<=n}, for positive n. This metric should be stated whenever the
minima 1,3,4,6,7 (n=1,...,5) are reported.

* A reduced strict CNF grammar for a finite nonempty language has acyclic
  nonterminal dependency. A productive dependency cycle adds at least one
  productive sibling at every step; pumping it gives unbounded lengths.
  Removing unreachable/unproductive symbols cannot increase the number
  of nonterminals. Thus acyclicity loses no optimum.
* After reduction, every nonterminal is reachable from the start. A
  topological order can therefore put the start last and put both children
  strictly before each binary-rule parent, exactly as in the encoding.
* The disjunction requiring a production for each nonterminal ensures
  productivity by induction in this order. Requiring it does not exclude
  any reduced optimum.
* Bounds 1<=maximum[A]<=n and maximum[A]>=maximum[B]+maximum[C] for each
  active A->BC prohibit all generated words longer than n by induction.
  Conversely the actual maximum generated length satisfies these bounds
  for a reduced grammar for the target: reachability with a productive
  context implies that every nonterminal's maximum length is at most n.
* Boolean membership equalities range over every binary word of lengths
  1,...,n and every split for a binary rule. Induction on the nonterminal
  order makes them exact. No epsilon or unit productions are allowed.
* The extracted grammar is independently expanded to its complete finite
  language and compared with the target, checking the SAT witness. A Z3
  `unknown` causes termination with no claimed minimum; earlier UNSAT
  results are required before reporting the first SAT minimum.

No flaw in the acyclicity restriction or length constraints was found.
The stored numerical minima rely on Z3's UNSAT answers, without separately
exported UNSAT proof certificates. They are exact finite solver results,
not an asymptotic claim and not evidence establishing context-freeness or
non-context-freeness of Q.

The saved SAT witnesses were also checked independently by fixed-point
expansion, without using the solver membership variables or the generator's
topological expansion. Their complete accepted-language sizes at n=1,...,5
were respectively 2,4,10,22,52, and every language equaled the intended
primitive-word target.

## 5. Hamming distance two and critical coordinates

The following strengthening was proposed by the root and independently
audited here.

**Lemma.** If two distinct nonprimitive words of length n have Hamming
distance exactly two, then both are squares.

**Proof.** Choose prime exponents p,q for the two words. If p=q, differences
repeat in p identical blocks, so their number is divisible by p. Therefore
p=q=2. If p!=q, put g=n/(pq), d=n/p and e=n/q. As in the puncturing proof,
the residue-incidence graph has components K_(p,q), with one edge for each
position. Give the two vertex classes the respective letters of the two
words. The mismatching positions are precisely edges with unequal endpoint
labels. Deleting those two edges makes every remaining connected component
monochromatically labeled.

If p,q>=3, deleting at most two edges cannot disconnect K_(p,q), so no
mismatch is possible. Hence one exponent is 2; say p=2 and q>=3. In
K_(2,q), a two-edge deletion disconnects the graph only when it isolates
one of its degree-two vertices. A single deleted edge does not disconnect
it. Thus both mismatches lie in one such component and isolate a single
degree-two vertex. The two degree-q vertices remain connected and have
equal labels. In every other component all labels agree as well. These
degree-q vertices encode the q-th-power word; their equality in each
residue class modulo g means that word has period g, hence period n/2.
It too is a square, while the other word was already a square. QED.

**Corollary.** A primitive word has at most two nonprimitive neighbors
obtained by changing one coordinate. In particular it has at most two
coordinates at which any one-coordinate change can make it nonprimitive.

The puncturing lemma permits at most one bad value per coordinate. If bad
neighbors occur at two different coordinates, the neighbors have Hamming
distance two and are squares by the lemma. A word one coordinate away
from a square has exactly one unequal antipodal pair, and its square
neighbors are obtained by changing either endpoint to the value at the
other endpoint. No third coordinate can give a square neighbor. Any
additional nonprimitive neighbor would, paired with either existing one,
have to be a square too, a contradiction. This argument also covers
arbitrary alphabets, including positive integers as run-length symbols.

The corollary concerns coordinate substitutions. It does not by itself
implement or contradict the quantifiers of the CFL pumping/Ogden lemmas.

## 6. Recursive-factor route: exact missing step

The polynomial rectangle covers in Section 1 are not a recursive grammar
construction. In particular the row predicates

    f(x) is undefined,
    f(x) is defined and f(x)_j=a

refer to the unique completion having some prime power exponent >=11.
The proof does not produce finite grammars for these predicates, bound
their grammar sizes, or express them by a recursively closed family of
factor languages. Restricting Q by a fixed context retains a rectangle
cover, but an arbitrary rectangle factor need not itself be such a
context restriction; replacing factors by restrictions silently assumes
an unproved closure property.

Conversely, no lower bound for grammar-compatible factors has been proved.
One would need an additional invariant involving their shared recursive
productions, not merely the number of unrestricted rectangles. Treating
this missing invariant as automatic would restate the original difficulty.

## 8. SCC countermodels and a sharp bound for coprime-count mechanisms

### Nonlinear recursion does not force an accepted power

The one-nonterminal grammar

    S -> a S S | b

is nonlinear and its dependency graph is strongly connected. Every word
it generates has #b-#a=1, by induction on the derivation tree, and therefore
is primitive: a proper-power exponent would divide this difference.

The grammar

    E -> epsilon | a E b E | b E a E
    S -> E a E

generates exactly the language D_1={w:#a-#b=1}. The E language is precisely
the balanced-count language. For completeness, a nonempty balanced word
can be split at its first return to signed count zero to apply one of the
two recursive E rules. A word with signed count one can be split at a
step from zero to one into a balanced prefix, a, and a balanced suffix.
Thus the S language is exactly D_1, which consists of primitive words.
The productive usable nonterminal E itself generates arbitrarily large
powers; the start context protects primitivity by imposing count difference
one. Therefore neither branching SCCs nor powers at a usable nonterminal
alone establish an accepted power.

For any fixed c>=1, the rule S_c -> E(aE)^c generates exactly
D_c={w:#a-#b=c}. To see completeness, take the last upward crossings of
levels 0,...,c-1 in a signed-count walk ending at c; the intervening gaps
have signed count zero.

Let R_j denote words whose length is congruent to j modulo 4. The CFL

    H = D_1 union (D_2 intersect R_0) union (D_4 intersect R_2)
        union {a,b,ab,ba}

consists entirely of primitive words. For odd lengths the first component
has consecutive letter counts. At n=4t the second has counts 2t+1,2t-1,
whose gcd divides 2 and is odd, hence is 1. At n=4t+2>=6 the third has
counts 2t+3,2t-1, whose gcd divides 4 and is odd, hence is 1. The explicit
finite patch handles lengths one and two. Fixed count differences and
regular intersections are CF, and finite union preserves CF. Central
binomial estimates give

    |H intersect {a,b}^n| = Theta(2^n/sqrt(n))

as n tends to infinity, including every length. Thus full binary exponential
growth at every large length does not force a CFL to contain a proper power.
The density here tends to zero; this is not a density-one counterexample.

### Semilinear coprime-count subsets have density O(n^(-1/2))

**Lemma.** Let T be a semilinear subset of N^2 consisting only of pairs
(x,y) with gcd(x,y)=1. Every linear component in any exact finite-union
representation of T has period vectors of rational rank at most one.

**Proof.** Suppose a component has base b and two linearly independent
nonnegative integer period vectors v,w. Choose a prime p not dividing
det(v,w). The matrix with columns v,w is invertible modulo p, so there
are t,s in {0,...,p-1} with b+tv+sw=(0,0) modulo p. This point belongs to
the component. It is nonzero, because b already belongs to T and all
vectors and coefficients are nonnegative. Its two coordinates are
divisible by p, contradicting gcd=1. QED.

Each rank-one component lies on an affine line whose nonzero direction
has positive coordinate sum. Consequently it meets x+y=n in at most one
point. A rank-zero component is a singleton. If K is the number of linear
components, T contains at most K Parikh vectors at any length n.

**Corollary.** If a binary CFL L contains only words whose two letter
counts are coprime, then

    |L intersect {a,b}^n| <= K binomial(n,floor(n/2)) = O(2^n/sqrt(n)).

Parikh's theorem supplies T and K, and the binomial bound counts all words
with each possible Parikh vector. The order of this bound is attained by
the preceding H example.

In particular the bound applies to any CFL subset of Q whose membership
depends only on letter counts. Indeed, including a nonzero vector with
gcd k>=2 would include the nonprimitive word
(a^(x/k)b^(y/k))^k; the zero vector is forbidden by epsilon not being
primitive. Such a language must therefore use only coprime vectors.

**Exact limitation.** An arbitrary primitive word need not have coprime
letter counts: aabb is primitive with counts (2,2). A general CFG for Q
may use order information and accept primitive words at such vectors
while rejecting their proper-power rearrangements. Nothing proved here
forces the Parikh image of a general CFL subset of Q into the coprime
pairs. In fact Q's Parikh image has full-rank semilinear components.
A theorem that every CFL subset of Q has density tending to zero would
resolve the original question negatively; no such theorem has been proved
by this argument.

## 9. Subsequent constructive-route audits

### Unbordered factors and Lyndon words

The equality Q=Sigma union cyc(T), for T={uv:u,v nonempty unbordered and
u!=v}, in `recursive_constructive_notes.md` is correct. If uv were an
r-th power of period d with r>=2, unborderedness forces both factor lengths
to be <=d. Therefore r=2 and both factors equal the period block, contrary
to their inequality. Conversely standard Lyndon factorization applied to
the least rotation of a primitive word of length >=2 supplies the two
distinct Lyndon, hence unbordered, factors. Sigma supplies exactly the
length-one cases, and epsilon is absent throughout.

The elementary unbordered splitting proof also checks out. A longest
proper nonempty unbordered prefix exists because the first letter works.
If the remainder had a proper nonempty border z, a border B of uz with
|B|<=|z| would border the whole word. A border B with |B|>|z| would give
u a nonempty proper border of length |B|-|z|: compare the portion before
the final z on both copies of B. Thus uz would be a longer proper
unbordered prefix, contradiction. The two factors cannot be equal since
their concatenation is unbordered. This includes words of length two;
their one-letter remainder simply has no proper nonempty border.

The pumping proof for the separated lexicographic comparator was checked
for every decomposition type, including pieces containing the separator,
pieces crossing a run boundary, changes in either a-run, and changes only
in one b-run. The length bound prevents simultaneous changes in the two
a-runs or the two b-runs. The consequent inverse-morphism proof that the
Lyndon language is not CF is valid. This is no negative result for Q,
because closure under cyclic shift has no converse of the needed kind.

A false explanatory endpoint-split claim in the direct-T discussion was
repaired: ababbb=ababb.b is a valid split with a one-letter final factor.
The four-run formula instead follows from the distinct middle-factor
construction and the impossibility of squares in T. For the six-run
formula, endpoint splits were handled separately. When all four proposed
inequalities fail, i<=k<=m and n<=l<=j. Removing the initial a leaves
border b if i=1 and border a^(i-1)b^n otherwise. Removing the final b
leaves border a if n=1 and border a^i b^(n-1) otherwise. Thus the formula
itself survives the correction. The primitive word ababaab, with unequal
endpoint letters, was also verified to be outside T.

Independent exhaustive checks verified the unbordered split and cyclic-T
equalities on all 8,190 binary words of lengths 1,...,12; the four-run T
formula for exponents 1,...,5; and the six-run formula for exponents
1,...,4. These are sanity checks supporting the audited proofs.

### Finite stratified cover of the scalar 2q slice

The final scheme in `finite_stratified_run_slices.md` was independently
audited. Its finite-cover identity is sound and complete, with normalized
odd-minus-even differences and the stated separate cases A(x)=0 and
A(x)!=0. Its linear-set schemes meet the stronger requirement of finitely
many bases, not merely finitely many period directions.

In the cancellation lemma, every greedy transfer uses opposite-sign
positions adjacent among the active positions. No later edge can have an
endpoint strictly inside an earlier edge, because all such positions were
already inactive when that edge was formed. Thus edges cannot cross.
Every original positive magnitude contributes to an edge or a leftover
singleton with positive integer coefficient. Taking a finite covering
subset H of those generators as the base, with at least one singleton,
is legitimate: subtracting one copy of each leaves nonnegative integer
coefficients. Conversely every enumerated base covers all coordinates
and has a nonzero signed surplus, which all allowed periods preserve.

In the F families, residual edges cannot cross any adjacent or outer edge
of M_0 or M_1. All-positive and all-negative cases use a single comparison
edge plus singleton periods; their bases realize exactly 1+t<2+t+u.
In each C family only its antipodal edge E and one noncrossing edge F
occur as matching periods. The new comparison edge shares an endpoint
with each, so no crossing is possible. The other coordinates are free
singleton periods. The cases cover all required strict inequalities.

The Ginsburg-Spanier application was checked against the primary source:
*Semigroups, Presburger Formulas, and Languages* (1966), Theorem 2.1 on
printed page 291, and constructive sufficiency Lemma 2.2. Its map extracts
the exponent vector from an ordered word over distinct letters, exactly
as required here. Source:
https://msp.org/pjm/1966/16-2/pjm-v16-n2-p09-p.pdf

The n-phase finite-state transducer then correctly emits one a per c_i
and one b at each phase advance, including empty coordinate blocks.
It converts the distinct-letter bounded CFL to
Q intersect (a* b)^(2q). The zero tuple is excluded, so it does not
introduce b^(2q), and epsilon is absent.

This completes the claimed fixed-slice theorem for each odd prime q.
It supplies no single grammar for unbounded q, no arbitrary run-pair
slice result, and no exact construction for Q. Neither this theorem
nor the unbordered characterization satisfies the original exact task.

## 10. Full-run transfer and limits of a cone obstruction test

### Scalar primitivity suffices for pair-valued run tuples

The root's following transfer has been independently audited. Fix m>=1
and let r be its number of distinct prime divisors. For nonnegative
integer vectors a,b of length m, the pair-valued tuple ((a_i,b_i)) is
primitive if and only if at least one scalar tuple

    a+t b, t in {0,...,r},

is primitive. For each prime p dividing m put d=m/p. If both a+t b and
a+s b have period d for two distinct t,s, subtracting shows that b has
period d, and then a does too. A primitive pair tuple forbids that, so
each prime can eliminate at most one candidate t. There are r primes and
r+1 candidates. Conversely any period of the pair tuple is a period of
every scalar projection. For m=1 there are no forbidden primes, and the
single candidate t=0 works.

Suppose the distinct-letter encoding of scalar P_m is CF. For each fixed
t, introduce distinct letters A_i,B_i,C_i and use the morphism

    h_t(A_i)=C_i,  h_t(B_i)=C_i^t.

Intersect h_t^(-1)(P_m's encoded language) with
A_1^+ B_1^+ ... A_m^+ B_m^+. The morphism adds the two run counts into
a_i+t b_i exactly. The finite union over t=0,...,r is precisely the
primitive pair-tuple language. Finally map every A_i to a and every B_i
to b. Positivity preserves the intended 2m alternating runs and unique
run parsing. All operations preserve CF; erasing B_i at t=0 in the inverse
morphism is permitted. Consequently scalar P_m being CF implies that
the full binary slice Q intersect (a^+ b^+)^m is CF.

Combining this with the finite scalar 2q cover establishes the full
2q-run-pair slice for each odd prime q, including m=6 and m=10. The attempted
negative route through the full 12-coordinate m=6 slice is therefore
blocked by this positive construction, not merely by an unsuccessful search.

The closure agent's independent mixed-case construction checks out too.
When neither scalar projection is primitive, the remaining pair-primitive
cases have one projection of period q but not 2, and the other of period
2 but not q. In the first orientation choose a_j!=a_(j+2), then use
b_(j+2)!=b_(j+q+2). In the reverse orientation choose b_j!=b_(j+2), then
a_(j+3)!=a_(j+q+3). On the flattened cyclic order the two comparison
edges have relative endpoints 0,4 and 5,2q+5, which do not cross for
q>=3. Each of the four sign choices for the two strict inequalities has
a stratified linear description using the two pair periods, one excess
singleton per pair, and free singletons elsewhere. No equality of whole
projection patterns has to be imposed on these safe families.

### Perfect matchings do not exhaust relevant stratified cones

On three coordinates the two periods

    (2,1,0), (0,1,2)

are stratified, and their sum is 2(1,1,1). Their span is the plane
x_1-2x_2+x_3=0. It contains neither a unit vector nor an equal-weight
two-coordinate vector e_i+e_j. Four disjoint such blocks on 12 coordinates
therefore give a stratified period set whose cone contains the all-ones
direction, but whose span contains no equal-weight pair or singleton
period whatsoever. An argument that extracts a noncrossing perfect
matching of equal-weight pairs from every such cone is invalid.

Affine intersection also need not imply an integer-cone intersection.
For example (1,0)+N(2,0)+N(0,2) has full affine span and contains a positive
all-ones direction in its period cone, but avoids x_1=x_2 by parity.

### A rigorous finite-cover criterion that clears lattice congruences

The following is a sufficient *test* for disproving a finite stratified
cover. No vector satisfying its hypothesis has been supplied for an
unresolved primitive slice.

Let S_1,...,S_k be rational linear subspaces of Q^n containing the all-ones
vector 1. Let x in N^n lie outside their union. Suppose that for every
finite stratified set H of nonnegative integer vectors for which 1 is a
strictly positive rational combination of all members of H, there is some
j such that

    x belongs to S_j + span_Q(H).                      (*)

Then N^n minus the union of the S_j is not a finite union of stratified
linear sets.

Proof: suppose a finite cover L(b_alpha;G_alpha) exists. Consider all
nonempty subsets H of all G_alpha with the stated positive-combination
property. There are finitely many. For each choose j and rational
coefficients witnessing (*). Choose a positive integer N clearing every
one of these finitely many denominators. Then for each such H there are
integers z_h with Nx+sum(z_h h) in its chosen S_j.

Every point Nx+t1, t>=0, avoids every S_j: subtract t1 and divide by N.
Some cover component contains infinitely many such points. Choose a
nonnegative integer generator-coefficient vector for each represented
point. Dickson's lemma supplies two distinct represented points with
coefficient vectors alpha<=beta. Removing zero generators if necessary,
their difference is k1 for some k>0, and equals a positive integer
combination of the generators H having beta_h>alpha_h. In particular
1 is a strictly positive rational combination of H.

Let y=Nx+t_0 1 be the earlier point. The denominator choice gives
y+sum(z_h h) in S_j. Add a sufficiently large multiple of
k1=sum((beta_h-alpha_h)h) so that every coefficient
z_h+M(beta_h-alpha_h) becomes nonnegative. This preserves membership in
S_j and produces a point of y+N H, hence of the original cover component,
contradicting that component's containment in the complement. QED.

The strict positivity requirement is essential: periods absent from the
chosen all-ones combination cannot automatically be shifted into the
nonnegative cone. The common dilation N addresses lattice obstructions;
a mere real affine-span computation without these steps does not prove
non-context-freeness.
