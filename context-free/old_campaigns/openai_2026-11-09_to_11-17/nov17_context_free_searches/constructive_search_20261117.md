# Additional constructive routes and an audit of finite projections

These are exact partial results and explicitly bounded experiments. No construction below is a CFG for all primitive words.

## Coprime letter counts are not a context-free certificate language

Let C be the binary language of words whose numbers of a's and b's have greatest common divisor one (with gcd(0,1)=1). Every word of C is primitive: the exponent of a proper power divides both letter counts. Nevertheless C is not context-free.

Here is a direct semilinearity proof. Any semilinear set S in N² has a positive integer P such that, for every fixed first coordinate x, its vertical fiber is eventually periodic with period P. To verify this, represent S as a finite union of linear sets. In each component and at fixed x, coefficients of generators with positive first coordinate have finitely many possibilities. What remains is a finite union of translates of the additive monoid generated by the second coordinates of vertical generators. A finitely generated submonoid of N is eventually periodic with period its positive gcd; an empty list contributes only a singleton. Taking a common multiple P of all positive vertical generators across the finite representation works for every fiber. The eventual threshold may depend on x, which causes no problem.

The set {(x,y):gcd(x,y)=1} has no such P. Choose a prime p not dividing P and fix x=p. Choose arbitrarily large y divisible by p. Then (p,y) is absent but (p,y+P) is present, contradicting eventual period P. Parikh's theorem excludes C from the context-free languages.

This blocks compiling the coprime-count sufficient test as an independent context-free sublanguage. It does not prove Q non-context-free: a non-context-free subset can be contained in a context-free language.

## Every fixed local-count horizon can miss primitive words

For K>=1 and t>=2, use the previously established primitive word

    W=(a^K b^K)^t (a^K b^(K+1))^t.

For every cyclic factor length ell<=K, every factor occurrence count is divisible by t. Indeed, the counts are

    count(a^ell)=2t(K-ell+1),
    count(b^ell)=t(2K+3-2ell),
    count(a^i b^(ell-i))=count(b^i a^(ell-i))=2t

for 1<=i<ell; all other factors have count zero. Thus even using coprimality of the counts of all factors up to a fixed length, rather than only letter counts or searching for a unique factor, misses arbitrarily long primitive words. Primitivity follows from the circular run-pair sequence A^tB^t, which has a unique A-to-B transition and hence cannot be a proper power.

## Deletion recursion

The assertion that every primitive word of length at least two has a primitive prefix or suffix of length one less is false. Its shortest binary counterexample is ababa: both endpoint deletions are alternating squares. The word itself is primitive because its length is prime and it is nonconstant. Exhaustive enumeration gives no shorter binary counterexample.

Allowing insertion at arbitrary positions does not preserve primitivity, even if every preceding word in the recursion is primitive: ab -> aba -> abab reaches a square. Insertion of a duplicate next to an existing letter also fails: abaab is primitive (counts 3 and 2), but duplicating its first a gives aabaab=(aab)^2. Thus neither of these local insertion operations supplies a sound unrestricted grammar rule.

Exhaustive binary enumeration through length 16 found that every primitive word of length at least two has some single-letter deletion that remains primitive. This is only finite evidence for a deletion property; even a proof of that property would not supply a sound insertion rule.

## Audit: reducing a fixed run count to its squarefree radical

Let m>=2, s=rad(m), g=m/s, and let x=(x_0,...,x_(m-1)) be a positive integer tuple. Partition it into s consecutive vectors of length g. For integer t>=0 define

    y_j(t)=sum_(r=0)^(g-1) t^r x_(gj+r),  0<=j<s,

where 0^0=1. Then x is primitive as a cyclic tuple if and only if y(t) is primitive for at least one

    t in {0,...,omega(m)(g-1)}.

For each prime p dividing m, maximal divisor period m/p equals g(s/p), so the tuple x has that period exactly when its vector sequence has period s/p. If x is primitive, for each p choose a pair of unequal vectors at that separation. Their scalar projection difference is a nonzero polynomial of degree at most g-1. It has at most g-1 real roots, hence at most that many candidate integer roots. The union of the selected bad sets has size at most omega(m)(g-1), leaving a candidate t at which every maximal divisor period fails. This y(t) is primitive.

Conversely, if x has period m/p for some p, then every y(t) has period s/p, so no projection is primitive. Positivity is maintained at t=0 because y_j(0)=x_(gj)>0.

For the bounded language P_m={A_0^x0...A_(m-1)^x(m-1): x positive and primitive}, each projection is induced by the morphism A_(gj+r) -> C_j^(t^r). Consequently P_m is the finite union, over these t, of the inverse images of P_s intersected with the regular positive-block-order language. Thus P_s context-free implies P_m context-free. Only inverse morphism, regular intersection and finite union are used.

The number of blocks m is fixed throughout this argument. It provides neither one grammar for unbounded m nor an upper bound independent of the number of distinct prime factors.

## Conjugate-factor candidate still unproved

A potentially sound operation is concatenation of two distinct conjugates of a primitive word. Exhaustive binary checks for factor length at most 12 found no case where the concatenation was a proper power. This is not asserted as a theorem here. Even if true, the operation requires a finite way to check primitivity and the conjugacy relation and therefore would not immediately yield a CFG.

## Distinct conjugate factors: proved sound, incomplete

The candidate above is true in a stronger form: if u and v are distinct conjugate nonempty words, uv is primitive, whether or not u itself is primitive.

Write u=xy and v=yx with x,y nonempty. If uv=xy²x were a proper k-th power, rotating the initial x to the end would show that y²x² is a proper k-th power. Apply the Lyndon–Schützenberger theorem: for nonempty words X,Y,Z and integers i,j,k>=2, X^iY^j=Z^k implies that X,Y,Z are powers of one common word. With X=y and Y=x this gives xy=yx, contradicting u!=v. Thus uv is primitive. The rotation of a power is a power: rotating r^k by a split of its root rotates each root copy identically.

This sound operation remains incomplete even after arbitrary rotations. Every result has even count of every letter, since conjugate factors have identical letter counts. The primitive word aaabbb therefore lies outside the resulting language: a proper power exponent would divide both counts 3, leaving only exponent 3, but the word is not an alternating cube.

Even among words with even letter counts the primitive word aaababbb is missed. It has length 8 and four copies of each letter. Its cyclic factors of length 4, in order of starting position, are

    aaab, aaba, abab, babb, abbb, bbba, bbaa, baaa.

Opposite halves at starts 0 and 1 (and their reverses at 4 and 5) have different letter counts. At start 2 the halves abab and bbaa are not conjugate: the rotations of abab are only abab and baba. At start 3 the halves babb and baaa have different letter counts. Thus no cyclic half split gives conjugate factors. The word is primitive: its length-4 halves differ and it has neither period 2 nor period 1; these exclude every proper divisor period of 8.

Finite recursion starting from a finite set of seed words and using only rotation and concatenation of distinct conjugates cannot generate all primitive words. Every operation after a seed doubles a length, so every generated length is 2^j times a seed length. In particular, only finitely many odd lengths occur; primitive words a^(n-1)b exist at every n>=2.

## Every nonconstant word admits a primitive deletion

Two distinct proper powers of the same length cannot differ in exactly one position. To prove this, rotate their common differing position to the final position, preserving their status as powers. Let their respective root lengths be p,q, both at most n/2. Their common prefix of length n-1 has periods p and q. Since

    n-1 >= p+q-gcd(p,q),

Fine–Wilf gives that prefix period d=gcd(p,q). The respective final letters are forced by periodicity to equal the prefix letters at positions n-p and n-q (one-based positions). These two positions are congruent modulo d, so the letters coincide, a contradiction.

Now let w be any nonconstant word of length n>=2. Choose adjacent unequal letters w_i and w_(i+1). Deleting w_i or deleting w_(i+1) yields two length-(n-1) words which differ in exactly one position. They cannot both be proper powers by the preceding lemma, so at least one is primitive. Both words are nonempty. For n=2 they are single letters and primitive directly.

This proves the earlier finite observation for all lengths, but also shows why deletion recursion alone cannot characterize primitivity: the same deletion property holds for every nonconstant proper power. For example abab deletes to the primitive word aba.

## Independent uniform-skeleton audit

The proof in tree_search_20261117.md passes the following checks. The auxiliary chain automaton can be constructed with disjoint internal copies of unary automata, so its paths represent exactly chains with unary side yields. There are only finitely many endpoint pairs; choosing one semilinear Parikh representation for each fixes all period weights independently of the eventual b-count m. Once an essential b-skeleton and endpoint labels are fixed, its chains are independent, making sums of chain linear components exact. The b-leaf intervals of skeleton nodes are laminar; their boundary coordinate pairs are nested, disjoint, or share endpoints, never crossing. Enforcing a zero final a-run discards positive-final bases and generators without introducing additional components because all coefficients are nonnegative. The proof therefore supplies its claimed grammar-dependent exponential component bound and linear total-base bound. It does not establish that primitive tuples violate these bounds.
