## Round-1 synthesis (lessons)
1. **Single-length combinatorics is dead.** Q∩Σⁿ is a union of O(n) balanced rectangles inside Q (G), and Q satisfies the interchange conclusion with loss 1/O(ω(n)) (B). Fooling sets have size 1 (C). Any negative proof must use relations ACROSS lengths (length-changing pumping) or the closure structure of a single grammar serving all lengths.
2. **Analytic methods are dead beyond unambiguity** without a theorem-strength lemma (D: a 2-ambiguous binary CFL already has a GF like Q's).
3. **Affirmative side:** the obstruction is combining refutations for unboundedly many primes with non-crossing comparisons whose distances can't be tied to n (E).
4. **Root-agent observation (to verify):** Q ∩ (a*b)⁶ looks CF. Via Ginsburg's theorem this is stratifiability of S₆ = ℕ⁶ \ (P₂ ∪ P₃). Two certificates, both conjunctions of a P₃-witness and a P₂-witness in nested non-crossing form, cover all of F = (P₂+P₃) ∩ S₆:
   - {x₁≠x₄ ∧ x₁+x₂≠x₃+x₄}
   - {x₁+x₂+x₃≠x₄+x₅+x₆ ∧ x₂+x₃≠x₄+x₅}

   So bounded slices with a fixed number of blocks are probably not where non-CF-ness lives.

## Round-2 synthesis
- Affirmative transversal UB: dead (UB is not CF). The remaining sufficient target is MP (minimal period > n/2).
- Bounded slices F_m: CF for m = 6 (proved) and likely for every m. Uniformity in m is equivalent to the full problem.
- OG(Q): reformulated without π or R. Evidence says it is true. The decisive open piece is Gap G (fully marked factors with no long periodic runs).
- Common core hit by B, E, H and I: a word that, after one local edit per candidate, becomes a q-th power for unboundedly many primes q, with Fine–Wilf failing at near-squares. No route found a sub-lemma weaker than the problem itself.
