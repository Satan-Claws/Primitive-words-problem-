Primitive words and polynomially ambiguous grammars

Can a context-free grammar generate exactly the primitive words while giving every word only polynomially many parse trees?

1. The problemWhat exactly is being asked

Primitive words

A word is primitive if it is not a repetition of a shorter word.

  • aab, abaab: primitive
  • abab = (ab)², aaa = a³: not primitive

Q = the set of primitive words over {a, b}. Almost every word is primitive; the non-primitive ones are rare but structured.

Ambiguity

A grammar may derive one word in many ways. G is polynomially ambiguous if every word w has ≤ C·|w|ᵏ parse trees, for constants C and k of the grammar.

Question: is there such a G with L(G) = Q?

Wich's dichotomy: every grammar is either polynomially or exponentially ambiguous, so "no" means "every grammar for Q is exponentially ambiguous".

Context. Whether Q is context-free at all is a famous open problem (Dömösi–Horváth–Ito, 1991). Known: Q is not unambiguous (Petersen 1996), and not finitely ambiguous (proved in this repo, Lean-checked). Polynomial ambiguity is the next rung on that ladder: it is strictly harder to rule out than finite ambiguity, and strictly easier than settling context-freeness.

3. What the idea isHow the picture evolved, and where it stands now

Primitivity is a "for every prime" statement

A word w is not primitive exactly when w = vᵖ for a prime p dividing gcd(#a, #b). So a proof that w is primitive must, for every such prime p, show that w is not a p-th power. For one fixed prime this is easy for a grammar: point to a single mismatch between positions i and i + n/p (linear ambiguity). The difficulty is doing it for all primes at once, with one grammar, for every length.

phase 1 · ajax

Force a blow-up on slices

Pick structured word families (slices with n = product of primes squared) where certifying every prime seemed to need many overlapping parse trees.

phase 2 · rounds 2–4

Slices are fine; typical words are hard

The slices can be covered with overlap 1 (audited). The real constraint is uniformity. Every simple certificate a grammar can check covers a vanishing fraction (~n−1/2) of random words.

phase 3 · rounds 5–15

The fold, and why it resists grammars

One certificate covers almost every word: the interval fold, made rotation-friendly as G⁻. Turning it into a grammar needs a length coupling across the centre. The current work is proving no unambiguous grammar can supply it.

The fold certificate in one picture

Fold the word in half. Compare the number of a's in the first k letters with the number in the last k letters, for every k up to n/2.

  • If w = uᵖ, the prefix and suffix of length ⌊p/2⌋·n/p are identical, so the counts are equal there. That position always lies between n/3 and n/2, for every prime p.
  • So "the counts differ at every k in [n/3, n/2]" proves primitivity, for all primes at once (Lemma F, audited). About 61% of words pass directly.
  • After a canonical rotation, almost every primitive word satisfies the stronger condition G⁻: prefix count < suffix count for every k ≤ n/2 (Lemma A, audited). Rotations cost nothing for grammars, since cyclic closure is exact.
Current working picture (after round 14). A grammar can generate the fold as nested letter pairs. What it can't obviously do is count along the fold. Lemma SA shows G⁻ is exactly a Dyck-type prefix times an a-heavy suffix, coupled by one length condition. Without that coupling, powers sneak in. Removing them is the original problem again, one level down (U1–U3). The negative programme now attacks the coupling directly. PR2 (audited) proves that a large class of unambiguous grammars cannot stay inside G⁻ while covering enough words. It even rules out ambiguity below n1/6, using a "measure squeeze": the words such a grammar is forced to make are too rare (n−2/3) for it to be injective. The one-sided case is now closed too (PR1s). Side mismatch is closed too (Theorem SM). One case remains before T8_unamb is a full theorem: two-tape models with unbalanced heavy tails.

7. Open questionsCurrent state after round 14, ranked by how directly they move the problem

1T8_unamb: no unambiguous grammar inside G⁻ is rotation-denseround 15 running

The sharpest live target. Any unambiguous grammar covering a positive share of rotations inside G⁻ must contain a critical, heavy-tailed block structure (Thm M, Lemma Bot). PR2 (audited) rules out the two-sided "level" case, and Prop HL shows every heavy block is level up to log slack. Three pieces remain:

  • Side mismatch: closed in round 15 (Theorem SM, written proof: a wrong-side block would force positive slack, making the grammar subcritical).
  • Heavier tails (exponent −1 − 2−k): balanced cases closed (Thm PR2β). Still open: unbalanced two-tape cases, one of them exactly borderline (N1/4 vs N1/4).
  • The one-sided case: closed in round 15 (Thm PR1s, written proof: Lemma CM truncates at √(m log m) and uses Hoeffding).
If T8_unamb fails (an explicit counterexample): an unambiguous grammar certifies most words, and the affirmative fold route revives.
If T8_unamb holds: a new theorem, and the first rigorous evidence that the dense fold certificate is beyond grammars. The next step would be lifting it from unambiguous to polynomial ambiguity, where PR2 already reaches n1/6.
2EQ-2q: match Q exactly on the rows #a = 2q

Is there a polynomially ambiguous grammar that agrees exactly with Q on every row with 2q a's (q prime)? If not, the problem is solved as "no". On these rows the only bad words are squares and q-th powers of two-a words. Lemma S characterises the good ones exactly via a gap invariant D. Theorem T shows no finite-automaton sorter works. Pumping b's keeps words on their row, which is the advantage, but the argument still needs the ambiguity bound to enter.

3P_cop: the minimal sandwich (now known to contain core 1)

Is there a polynomially ambiguous language containing every coprime-count word and only primitive words? Prop H (audited) makes it the weakest statement in the chain P(Q) ⇒ … ⇒ P_cop. Theorems N and N2 (both audited) show that "row" arguments (prime rows, rows 2q in containment form) can never refute it. So any proof that P_cop fails must also handle typical words.

4Lifting to polynomial ambiguity

Every negative tool so far works best for unambiguous grammars. PR2's squeeze already tolerates multiplicity up to n1/6−ε. A full lift needs either a better squeeze, or Wich's structure theory to control where ambiguity can sit (at most k·log n disjoint ambiguous nodes per tree).

5Older open forms (lower priority)
  • L₆: words of length 6m that are neither squares nor cubes. Its generating function is rational, so counting can't decide it.
  • Uniformity in depth k: the self-similar block reduction is exact but doesn't amplify.
  • SPREAD-JOINT / NONUNIF: the last per-slice gaps. They can't resolve the problem alone.

8. How this leads to a solutionThe routes as they stand now

Route to "yes"

  1. Find an unambiguous or polynomially ambiguous grammar inside G⁻ covering a positive share of rotations (this would refute T8_unamb).
  2. Patch the rare exceptions: the tie family, and two-prime cases such as L₆.
  3. Union with the uniform single-prime grammars; prove L(G) = Q for all lengths and bound the trees.

Evidence so far points the other way: 240k-grammar searches and every construction failed at the length coupling.

Route to "no"

  1. Finish T8_unamb (one case left: unbalanced heavy tails).
  2. Lift it to polynomial ambiguity (PR2 already reaches multiplicity n1/6).
  3. The hardest step: show any polynomially ambiguous grammar for Q must contain a dense fold-type sublanguage, or close P_cop / EQ-2q directly.

Step 3 is where the full difficulty lives. An argument that ignored ambiguity would prove Q isn't context-free, which is harder and not the target.
